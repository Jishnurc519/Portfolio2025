What is Gamma Barbecue?

We are a dj and graphic design duo who create audio visual experiences for ourselves and our clientele. I take care of the visual and audio production side of things and my partner Hrithwik Dhanpal covers the DJing and logistical side of things. We usually work in the space of music and dance. Creating synesthetic experiences is our passion, and telling the stories that we, along with our collaborators, want to convey to our audiences through these experiences is integral to our process of building the experience.

In the cinemas, the complex interactions between the physical and the digital systems create an experience where this recognition of either space is suspended. An entirely new experience is created through sounds and visuals — characters, contexts and stories emerge from the cohesive use of technology and physical (and metaphysical) materials. It’s this space, where new technology informs our story telling and expression and vice versa through performance, that I find myself interested in.

We love working with cool tech, old and new.

These are the base ideas that drive the aspects of performance behind Gamma Barbecue.

Origin story

Two stolen CRTs, and everything after them.

Throwback to 2023. Hrithwik Dhanpal — a photographer, DJ and cool guy — and I were sitting in a room messing around with this software we had found called TouchDesigner.

We had stolen a couple of those old CRT TVs we found lying on the road. After removing the smell of dog piss and reconnecting some wires, we were absolutely ecstatic when we saw one of them working.

We had anyway been working on trying to create a visual system that could recognise the kind of music that was playing and set the lights and projection content appropriately, for some of Hrithwik’s sets — when someone offered us money to do the same. That’s how we got our very first gig, and we haven’t looked back since. Except now.

Over the course of the next few years we experimented with more material that we found (stole), with more tech, and with an ever growing appetite for exploring the unexplored.

  1. MIDI

CR2s

CR2s is an installation that we carry around to perform for our own music, and for other musicians that we vibe with. It’s a fairly simple set up: two TVs, an audio processor and a DJing deck. And cables.

The setup allows us to control the visuals through the same intuition that one uses to mix different songs on a DJ deck — using concepts of faders, samplers and the different offerings that the equipment gives, and relating those to different parameters of our visualisations.

Music does not exist in a vacuum. It is made by people and for people. It serves as a powerful medium for expression, storytelling and dance, capturing the rhythms of life and the nuances of human experience.

We love working with musicians. Through our installations and set design we hope to be able to convey the stories that they want to tell.

Having visuals be audio reactive creates a driving force on a dance floor, a call to action. The combination of curated visuals interacting with sounds and camera feed, along with the music, allows for subjective experiences while tapping into the human tendency to move to the pulsing beats.

Digital Repaints

Digital Repaints is a series where I explore using the characteristics of the forms and materials of a real life subject (such as this painting by V.S. Gaitonde) to dictate the nature of the visuals that are generated. By superimposing the two and allowing the work to interact with the viewer, the agency that we grant the viewer to be an integral part of the nature of the piece helps in creating an experience that transcends the physical and the digital.

Messing around with blending modes and transparencies can lead to some very interesting results.

Abhyu

Abhyu is a sound engineer from Chennai, India. He makes liminal minimalist soundscapes. His music aims to offer a sonic space that invites listeners to question conventions and embrace alternative narratives and perspectives that disrupt hegemonic discourses. The focus is on creating new sonic possibilities, encouraging listeners to embrace the nomadic and the unpredictable.

The following pieces were created as covers for two of his songs, Superlinear and Intrication. To him, “the images lend a ‘Polar’ atmosphere without resorting to obvious polar symbols, which is something I aim to achieve sonically as well.”
